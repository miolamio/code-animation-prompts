## Style prompt

Georgian satirical print: a copperplate engraving, hand-coloured. Brown-black line only: swelling burin outlines about 1/400 of frame width, heavier on the shadow side; hatching follows each form and covers every surface (spacing about 1/250 of frame width), cross-hatched in the darks, ruled on flat areas. Colour covers nearly the whole picture, clear but strong: washes spilling a few pixels off the line in claret #B3262E, brass #D8A93A, flesh #F0C4A4, slate blue #6D8AA6, warm ochre and umber on large surfaces. Sepia laid paper, foxing, plate mark, engraved border. Stage it like William Hogarth: big-nosed caricatures, bystanders or comic props and animals around the subject; one slanted handwritten speech balloon in the user's language. Camera: hold the full plate 2 s, whip in, glide to another detail, close-up. Avoid: even mechanical hatching, blank paper, colour exactly inside lines, muddy colour.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Hogarth copperplate line engraving, Rowlandson/Gillray hand-coloring, satirical speech bubble

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Tom Jones: a gold-trimmed tricorn hat, a lipstick kiss mark on its face, a chicken leg in hand. No shot-reverse-shot; one lateral slide instead: from a two-shot across the table, sliding to Mrs. Waters winking as a speech bubble pops up. Then a cut to a close shot: heart-shaped eyes, a swoon and a sway, then the tricorn taken off and clapped toward the camera so the frame goes fully black, and the next part opens out of the black.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. A whip-in from the left to a two-shot across a table laden with food, Clawd (Tom) tearing at a chicken leg; the camera slides across the tabletop to land on Mrs. Waters, who pops up a speech bubble (Another Leg, Mr Jones?) and winks. (1.3s)
2. Close shot of Clawd: its eyes turn into hearts, it sways blissfully, takes another small bite, then takes off the tricorn and claps it toward the camera, the picture going black; slow push-in. (1.2s)
