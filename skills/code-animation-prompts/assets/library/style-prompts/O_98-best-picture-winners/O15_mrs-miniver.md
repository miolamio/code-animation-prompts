## Style prompt

A 1942 British wartime stone-lithograph poster. Simple, strong shapes from three plates, 1-3 px off register: a colour plate with all flat fills; a grey-blue crayon plate darkening every shadow with short slanted stone grain; a green-black key line about 1/500 of frame width around every shape, figures and clouds included. Palette by role: cream #EBDFC2 paper and highlights, grey-blue #8497A6 sky and shadow, brick red #A23F2D and deep green #34503F as accents, at most a quarter of the frame; browns and skin mixed from cream and brick; key #1E2422. Scenes may be full. Airbrushed skies; only the paper grain boils at 12 fps. Pale light bands, angled from the source, cancel the shadow plate. Figures stand frontally. Camera: slow drifts, a tilt up. References: Frank Newbould, Abram Games. Avoid: dot screens, clean vector edges, big saturated fields.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Mrs. Miniver: a lace-trimmed straw hat, a hymn book and the red "Mrs. Miniver" rose. In the wide shot it sings in the choir; on the cut to the close shot it hears engines, stops and looks up; the third shot is its line of sight, watching a Spitfire formation fly past through the hole in the roof. Three hard cuts, joined by the single motion of looking up.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Wide shot looking along the bombed church nave: villagers stand among the pews singing, sunlight falling in shafts; Clawd in a lace-trimmed straw hat sits in the front row singing, holding the hymn book and a red rose. The camera drifts back slightly. (0.8s)
2. Close shot of Clawd and the rose: the sound of engines arrives, it stops singing and looks up at the roof. A gentle push-in. (0.6s)
3. A vertical low-angle shot from inside the church up through the hole in the roof: broken rafters frame the sky as Spitfires fly over in a V formation. The camera pushes in slowly with a slight rotation. (1.1s)
