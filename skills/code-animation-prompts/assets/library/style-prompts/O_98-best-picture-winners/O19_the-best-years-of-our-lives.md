# O19 The Best Years of Our Lives

- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · Color and War 1939–1949
- Looks like: a 1946 picture-magazine photojournalism spread: coarse black halftone dots on coated paper, a vertical gutter fold, overprinted captions. Stripped B-17s stretch to the horizon in deep focus; Clawd is printed alone on a spot-orange plate and is the only color.
- Style: picture-magazine photojournalism halftone spread
- Keywords: 2.5D·B&W
- Year: 1946

## Style prompt

Each frame is a 1946 picture-magazine photo spread. Render the scene as a grey photo (3D or painted): low sun, long shadows, deep focus (near and far both sharp). The photo bleeds across both pages, crossed by a soft vertical gutter crease. Print as a 45° black halftone, dot pitch about 1/250 of frame width, ink #1A1A1A on off-white coated stock #ECE6D8. A short black headline and caption sit in a quiet area. Only the hero the story centres on, at least 1/4 of frame height, prints on its own spot-orange plate (e.g. #D97757) at another angle, 2-3 px off, black only in its deepest shadows. Camera: walk in, push to a detail, pull back to reveal an open magazine. References: cinematographer Gregg Toland; *Life* post-war photo essays. Avoid: the gutter cutting the hero, coarse dots that break the photo. Slight ink gain. The headline and caption are overprinted from the first shot.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

45-degree coarse halftone dots, Gregg Toland deep focus, 1946 picture-magazine spread layout, spot-orange plate

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Fred: a captain's garrison cap and the wool collar of a B-3 flight jacket. It walks along the corridor of bombers and stops on the beat to look up; cut into the nose compartment it is looking at, then pull back a long way to find that the whole picture is a page in a 1946 magazine. Two hard cuts, and the last shot is one continuous pull back.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Low camera position, deep-focus wide shot of the aircraft graveyard: Clawd in a garrison cap with his wool collar turned up walks under the wing of a stripped B-17, stops, and looks up at the glass nose; the camera moves right and pushes in. (0.9s)
2. Looking out from the bombardier's glass nose compartment: rows of bombers stretching toward the distant hills, Clawd at the left edge of the frame; slow push-in. (0.8s)
3. Pull back: the photograph shrinks into an open magazine, headline GRAVEYARD OF THE FORTRESSES, lying on a dark tabletop. (0.8s)
