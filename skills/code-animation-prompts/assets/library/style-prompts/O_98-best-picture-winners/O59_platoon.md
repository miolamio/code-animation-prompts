## Style prompt

English:

Reportage pen-and-wash field sketch. Pen: warm black, fast confident lines ~1/600 of frame width, flicked ends, overshooting corners; quick hatching and zigzags for shadow, on figures and ground. Bold transparent washes with soft wet edges, blooms and uneven pigment (not flat polygons) cover about 70% of the frame, shifted 2–4 px (at 1920×1080) off the line. Colours: olive #70783E, ochre #CEA054, smoke grey #706C70, one hot accent #F0822E for sky and light sources; skin in diluted ochre; sketchbook paper #F2ECDE. Bare paper only in highlights, a thin edge band and brush gaps; light grain. A scrawled pencil note (place, time, weather) in a corner, 1/40 of frame height, in the user's language. Layer for lateral tracks; hard cuts like turning a page. Reference: US Army Vietnam Combat Artists Program (1966–70). Avoid: polished finish; empty page; muddy washes; heavy noise.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Elias: a boonie hat and dog tags. Two hard cuts go from the ground to the air and back to the ground, each shot like another page in a sketchbook; at the last shot both arms snap out into a poster pose, then sag slightly as the helicopter lifts away. The scene plays at 1.1x speed.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Lateral move following a patrol advancing through elephant grass; Clawd, in a boonie hat and dog tags, stands in the middle as soldiers walk past him. (0.8s)
2. Hard cut to a view down from the door of a Huey: a burning landing zone, grass flattened by the rotors and the helicopter's own shadow, Clawd small in the clearing; slow pull-back. (0.8s)
3. Hard cut to a close shot of Clawd, returning to the poster composition: under napalm-orange firelight he throws both arms into a V shape, soldiers run past on either side, and the helicopter lifts off. (0.9s)
