## Style prompt

18th-century cut-paper silhouettes. Each figure: a solid black profile #0B0705 with its own cut detail (hair, nose, lace holes, props), a 2–3 px pale-gold rim #F7E3A0 (at 1920×1080) toward the nearest flame, and hairline gold lines along clothing folds. Gilt relief #B88A2E and red velvet #6E0F14 frame the scene like an opera box; an outdoor subject becomes a lit painted backdrop on a stage, cut-paper figures in front. Reds and golds fill most of the frame; night blue #0C1A3A only for the far sky. Only the hero gets a painted, coloured face. Candles flicker gently (±5%), warm halos about five flames wide; light haze, vignette, fine grain. Signature shot: silhouettes framing a bright stage far back; slow lateral glide, then push. References: silhouettists Edouart, Miers; cinematographer Miroslav Ondříček (*Amadeus*, 1984). Avoid: plain black blobs; copy-pasted profiles; blue walls; strobing flames. Set them in a candlelit Rococo interior.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

18th-century silhouette portraits, Rococo gilded relief, candlelight bloom, Schinkel's starry-sky stage set

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Mozart: a white powdered wig with a pink ribbon, a lace cravat, and a baton keeping time to the music. One camera move rises from the orchestra pit to the stage, landing exactly on the Queen of the Night's high note, then hard cuts to Salieri watching from the dark. 1:1 time, no slow motion.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Lateral move along the candlelit orchestra pit to Clawd conducting with a baton, then rising past him and pulling back to land on the starry stage, where the Queen of the Night raises her arm and sings the high note. (1.6s)
2. Hard cut to Salieri's dark box: in a row of silhouettes, only his face is drawn in, lit from below by a single candle; slow push-in. Clawd is not in the frame. (0.9s)
