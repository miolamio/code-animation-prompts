- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · New Hollywood 1970–1979
- Looks like: magazine illustration in the line of Leyendecker and Rockwell: lit planes, parallel brushstrokes, crisp dark contours. The film's chapter cards imitate *Saturday Evening Post* covers, and the 1936 betting parlor is drawn the same way.
- Style: *Saturday Evening Post* illustration
- Keywords: 2D

## Style prompt


---

English:

1930s Saturday Evening Post cover illustration. Realist figures, correct proportions, expressive faces, built from blocky planes (lit, shadow, warm bounce from below) filled with blended strokes about 1/150 of frame width, varied in length, following the form, never stripes. Dark contour thicker on the shadow side, 1/800–1/300 of frame width. Warm key from upper left. Palette: plaster #E2C492, oak #764626, slate #28322C, brass #E2B046, ink #341E18; oak and ink darks about a third of the frame; sky plaster cooled with slate, water light slate, skin plaster plus brass. Grainy board (±3%), warm vignette. Default one-point perspective, period detail, haze, secondary figures if the story has any. Each figure is drawn once and moves whole (breathing, sway, sliding along a path). Camera: one long, smooth push in. References: J. C. Leyendecker, Norman Rockwell. Avoid: stiff cut-out figures, even outlines, muddy colour.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

*Saturday Evening Post* cover illustration, Leyendecker-style plane modeling, Jaroslav Gebr's chapter cards

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Hooker: a light fedora, a red bow tie, suspenders. The whole piece has no cuts, only one push-in. When the push arrives it gives the con men's signal (a finger alongside the nose), then pulls down its hat brim and winks at the camera, as if drawing the audience into the con.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. One continuous take: from a wide shot of the betting parlor (an odds board, betting windows, Gondorff in a window) pushing all the way in to Clawd as Hooker, who puts a finger to the side of its nose as the signal, then tips its hat brim down toward the camera. (2.5s)
