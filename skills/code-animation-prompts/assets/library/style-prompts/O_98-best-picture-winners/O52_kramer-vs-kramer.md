## Style prompt

A careful 6–8-year-old's wax-crayon drawing on white paper. Crayon catches the paper grain's peaks; light pressure leaves 40–60% paper showing, heavy pressure fills it; overlaps show through. Outlines in black or dark-brown crayon, waxy, wobbling, about 1/300 of frame width. Fills are back-and-forth scribbles, strokes about 1/40 of frame width long and 1/250 wide, rows overshooting the outlines, each area in its own direction, spacing and angle uneven, never a regular mesh. Primary box: red #E0382C, orange #F28A1E, yellow #F7D23A, green #3AA345, blue #2E6BD8. Child conventions: a ground line, flattened perspective, arrowed labels, a smiling sun, one mirrored letter (in Chinese, a reversed radical), patterns coloured cell by cell. Text in the user's language, in wobbly crayon strokes, not a typeface. Camera: shaky close-ups, then a calm drift. Avoid: neat adult rendering, random scrawl, uniform stipple or mesh, smooth gradients.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Crayon on textured paper, how a 6-8 year old draws, handwritten labels

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Ted: a blue apron with the strings tied, a spatula in hand; Billy stands on a chair cracking eggs. The first and last scenes use the same pan as a match cut, telling the whole father-and-son arc in a single jump cut. Overall about 1.35x speed; the cut into the wide shot lands just as the toast is caught, followed by a little hop and a glance at the camera.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Big close-up of the frying pan from the first attempt: burnt toast, black crayon smoke, eggshell; handheld shake, slowly pulling back. (0.7s)
2. Match cut to the same pan at the end, perfectly clean: a golden slice of toast flips up out of it. (0.4s)
3. Under the caption "ME AND DADDY MAKE FRENCH TOAST", father and son stand side by side: Clawd catches the toast with the spatula, Billy bursts into a smile, a red heart pops up, and Clawd does a proud little hop; the camera drifts up to the crayon skyline outside the window. (1.4s)
