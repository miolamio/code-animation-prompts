## Style prompt

A 1936-39 WPA (Federal Art Project) silkscreen poster. Six opaque inks printed light to dark, each later screen cut away around lighter shapes (clouds, sun): cream #F0E2C0 for light, ochre #E0A842 and vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm planes, teal #22686E for water and shade, the hero's own ink (e.g. #D97757, only on the hero the story centres on), brown keyline #322018 last. Shadows: a darker ink overprinted, never grey. Big areas get split-fountain blends: two inks in 2-3 coarse steps, the middle one their overprint. Screens 1-3 px off register; wandering stencil edges, squeegee streaks, warm rag-paper grain (±5%) on every flat. Figures fill 1/2-2/3 of frame height: big planes, crescent shadows, expressive faces. A bold capital title band, user's language. Slow pushes; screens pop in one by one. Avoid: halftone dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Grandpa Vanderhof: round metal-framed glasses, a harmonica, a cane leaning against the chair. A houseful of people each doing their own thing, while the fireworks in the basement grow bigger and bigger in the last shot. Two hard cuts: living room → harmonica → fireworks.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. The whole Vanderhof living room, like a single poster: Clawd as Grandpa, in round glasses, sits in a teal armchair playing the harmonica, Essie dances on tiptoe by the window, Kolenkhov shouts out the beat, and De Pinna pokes his head up through the basement trapdoor; gentle pull back. (0.8s)
2. Close shot of Clawd: round glasses, harmonica, musical notes drifting upward, De Pinna at the edge of the frame; push in. (0.6s)
3. Back to the living room as the fireworks in the basement go off: rockets, starbursts and smoke columns fill the house; the camera pulls back. (1.1s)
