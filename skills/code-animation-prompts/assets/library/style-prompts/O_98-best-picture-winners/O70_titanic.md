# O70 Titanic

- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · Turn of the Century 1990–1999
- Looks like: White lines on Prussian-blue blueprint paper rise up and grow perspective, then develop into a full-color 3D ship at sunset, running on into the night. The ship itself is the film's emblem, so the shipyard drawings and the live scene use the same model.
- Style: blueprint developing into a 3D scene
- Keywords: 3D
- Year: 1997

## Style prompt

A blueprint turning into photoreal-leaning 3D. Opening only (1–2 s, under 15% of the film): white edge lines about 1/800 of frame width on Prussian-blue paper (#12306B to #244D8F), hidden lines dashed, figures and terrain as contours, uneven exposure, folds, lettering. A dolly-zoom stands the elevation up into perspective; colour spreads from the hero along a soft warm edge, ghost lines fading within a second. Then the signature look: golden-hour real 3D, low sun, amber highlights #F2B860, warm shadows #3A2530, sun glitter on water, soft mist, shallow focus, gentle bloom, fine grain; then blue hour and night, warm lights coming on. One unbroken move: push in and stand up (blueprint), then, in equal shares, drift low past the hero, rise and orbit, descend. Reference: cinematographer Russell Carpenter (*Titanic*, 1997). Avoid: lingering on the drawing; hard cuts; flat CG light.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Shipyard general arrangement blueprint, three.js ship modeled at true scale, Russell Carpenter's color, single continuous take

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Jack, with a navy wool scarf around his neck. One shot of 16 beats with no cut: the blueprint finishes unfolding in 1.5 seconds and finishes developing in 2.4 seconds, with Clawd spreading his arms beside Rose; sunset to night is a time-lapse within the camera move, the iceberg appears in the final two seconds, and Clawd turning to raise the alarm lands on the last few beats.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Single continuous take: push toward the bow over the general arrangement plan; the line drawing stands up into perspective and develops outward from the two figures into a sunset; a low camera position close to Rose's backlit silhouette, with Clawd spreading his arms beside her; the camera rises and circles around the port bow, the sun sinks and the ship's lights come on; it passes over the forecastle into the night as Rose climbs down from the railing; then it descends behind Clawd as the iceberg emerges from the sea mist and Clawd turns and raises an arm to sound the alarm. (6.7s)
