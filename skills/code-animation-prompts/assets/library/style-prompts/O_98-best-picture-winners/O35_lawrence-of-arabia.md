## Style prompt


---

English:

1960s hand-painted epic film poster, gouache with oil highlights. Draw forms solidly, then paint: an opaque mid-tone base, directional strokes following each form (1/80–1/50 of frame width on large areas, smaller on faces and hands), soft-blended edges; model every form in three values; dry-brush only on dust, cloth and distant figures; sharp warm-cream highlights last on lit edges. Warm–cool contrast: cobalt sky #1F4D97 to pale blue #A9C3D6, warming to cream near a low sun; ground #EEC27A (any terrain); ochre-red #B56A33 for skin, earth and costume; violet #6C6394 for distant shadows. The hero centred and largest, a diagonal gesture, cloth streaming; smaller, looser figures may recede behind. Light board grain (±3%), warm vignette, haze. Motion: bounce, slow push to a low close-up, a whip pan out. Avoid: scratchy uniform hatching, flat digital fills, cold-only colour.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

gouache and oil poster painting, Howard Terpning 1962 poster, Freddie Young 70mm desert light

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Lawrence: white robes, a white headcloth, a gold headband, riding a camel and raising a scimitar. The opening is the most famous match cut in film history: the flame of the match from the previous part becomes the desert sunrise. The whole charge runs at a constant 1.5x speed, the scimitar raised on the beat with a glint at its tip, and the last shot whip-pans out of frame, leading into *Tom Jones*.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Big wide shot of the desert at dawn: a huge sun rises through the heat haze dead center in the frame, a match cut with the match blown out in the previous part; slow push-in. (0.7s)
2. Poster composition from a tracking vehicle: Clawd (Lawrence) on a camel, charging in among the cavalry, the scimitar held low at first, then raised on the beat with a glint at its tip; bumpy shake, slow push-in. (1s)
3. A low-angle push toward the raised scimitar, then a whip pan to the right out of frame, leading into the next part. (0.8s)
