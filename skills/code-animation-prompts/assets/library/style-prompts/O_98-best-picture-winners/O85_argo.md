## Style prompt


---

English:

1970s Jack Kirby comic page. Heavy tapered brush contours, feathered spot blacks, exaggerated perspective, Kirby Krackle energy bubbles, block sound effects, hand-lettered balloons and yellow captions. CMYK flats printed as 45° Ben-Day dots: cyan #1696CD, magenta #DE2C70, yellow #FFD616, black #1C161A on aged newsprint #EEE2C2, colour plates 1 px off register. Paper grain, browned edges, a gutter fold shadow, warm lamp falloff. Lay the story out in panels and move between them: push into the splash, pan along a strip. Dots stay round and grow into pop-art dots on push-ins. Avoid: smooth digital gradients, clean white paper, stiff tube-like machines.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Jack Kirby cosmic comics, Ben-Day four-color dot printing, yellowed newsprint, Lord of Light concept art

### Director's notes

Clawd plays the comic's space hero: a red-lined high collar, a blue cape, gold bracers, and a Kirby-style ray gun in hand. The beam fires in the first beat, then two hard cuts turn the page from the fake epic to the real escape, and finally Clawd sheds the costume and becomes one passenger behind a cabin window. Three panels in 2.5 seconds, like reading a comic.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Medium shot of a big cosmic double-page panel, slow push-in: Clawd the caped space hero stands on a rock ledge firing the ray gun, the beam blowing up a robot's reactor, "KRAKA-THOOM". (0.9s)
2. Hard cut to the panel with the red film strip: several hostages in a terminal and the narration "THE MOVIE WAS FAKE. THE MISSION WAS REAL.", the camera drifting slightly right. (0.8s)
3. Hard cut to the steel-blue night runway panel, moving laterally from left to right past the pursuing trucks and jeeps and stopping at the airliner lifting off the ground; in the small porthole panel in the corner, Clawd sits behind the window looking back. (0.8s)
