# O39 A Man for All Seasons

- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · The Swinging Sixties 1960–1969
- Looks like: an old-master oil portrait: smooth glazed modeling, fur collars painted hair by hair, velvet highlights, gold chains rendered link by link, plus aged varnish and craquelure. Holbein himself painted Thomas More in 1527, and this uses his composition.
- Style: Holbein Tudor oil portrait
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1966

## Style prompt

Oil portrait after Hans Holbein the Younger. Paint in layers: a toned underlayer, directional strokes following light and shadow, soft glazes, fine detail last; smooth finish, brushwork faint in background and fabric. Render each material: fur hair by hair, velvet folds with sharp light ridges, metal with two highlights per piece, wool and leather with soft nap. Any subject becomes a formal half-length sitter in its own clothes; its setting shows through a window or in an object it holds. Palette: deep green curtain #1F4A26, black #141113, velvet red #6E0C15, gold #B0801E, fur brown #3E2818; warm, fully modelled skin. Soft light from upper left. Signature shot: the portrait against the curtain; then material close-ups, a slow pull back into a dark room lit by warm pools. Warm varnish, light craquelure. Northern Renaissance. Avoid: flat fills, hard digital edges, even light, unmodelled faces.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

old-master oil glazing, Holbein's *Sir Thomas More* (1527), candlelit Tudor interior

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Thomas More: a Tudor flat cap, black robes with a fur collar, red velvet sleeves, an SS gold chain, holding a letter sealed in red wax. Coming after the spinning meadow, this one is still: a single cut from a big close-up to the portrait, then a long pull back all the way; the candles are about to go out, and the darkening picture leads into the night of the next part.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Big close-up of the face, the SS gold chain and the Tudor rose pendant: Clawd (More) first looks to one side, then turns its gaze to the camera and blinks; slow push-in. (0.9s)
2. Holbein portrait composition (Tudor flat cap, fur collar, red velvet sleeves, green brocade curtain, the letter in hand): a solemn nod, the gaze falling to the letter, then a slow pull back to reveal the candlelit Tudor room; the candle flames are about to go out and the picture darkens. (1.6s)
