# O4 Cimarron

The film opens with the 1889 Oklahoma land run at full gallop, so it is shot with the look of photographs from that year, and this chapter also stays monochrome.

## Style prompt

A moving 1880s gold-toned albumen photograph. Paint soft-edged flat shapes by brightness: main subjects dark (15-30%), ground 50-60%, sky about 90%; visible hatching and grass tufts (lines ~1/300 of frame width). Then print: grayscale, slight blur (~1/800 of frame width), a faint smear of three horizontal offset copies growing with camera speed, multiply cream #F2DCB4, lift blacks to umber #2E1C0F, blotchy development (±5%), vignette to #785634, per-frame grain (±4%), dust, scratches. Hand-tint only the hero the story centres on (e.g. #D97757) as transparent dye, mask feathered 2-3 px; lamps stay sepia. Wide panorama: the middle band packed with repeated small elements (figures, animals, vehicles; for a lone subject fences, posts, trees), no big plane dominating. Camera: one 4-5 s ease-out pull back from the hero to the panorama. Avoid: hard ink outlines, colour beyond the hero, a flat grey print.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

gold-toned albumen print, Western panorama old photograph, hand-tinted figure

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Yancey Cravat: white wide-brimmed cowboy hat, neckerchief, riding a black horse at the head of the charge. The whole piece runs at 1.1x. First pull back to see more horses, then hard cut back to the close shot; around beat 5 he draws his revolver and fires at the sky.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Starting from a close shot of Clawd on his black horse leading the charge, pull all the way back to a wide shot: covered wagons, a stagecoach, a huge field of riders in the dust. (1.5s)
2. Cut to a close shot of Clawd on horseback: he raises his revolver and fires at the sky, the picture jolting with the recoil; gentle push-in. (1s)
