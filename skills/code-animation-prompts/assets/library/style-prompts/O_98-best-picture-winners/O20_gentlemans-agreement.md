## Style prompt

Graphite pencil like a 1940s magazine illustration, finished and controlled. The drawing builds in the first 1-1.5 s, then holds: faint perspective rays and head ovals, pressure-tapered contours (about 1/600 of frame width) overshooting at corners, then 1-4 near-parallel hatching passes (more for darker), about 1/300 of frame width apart, following the form, then rubbed tone and eraser lifts. The finished frame is toned nearly edge to edge: setting fully drawn in mid-greys, about 15% dark accents, only the outer tenth fading to loose margins. Paper #F2EEE5 with a light tooth; lead #2C2B2E. Only the hero the story centres on wears coloured pencil (e.g. #D97757) on its clothing; skin stays graphite. Faces carry the drama. Camera: a slow ease out, then cuts to close-ups. References: illustrators Al Parker and Robert Fawcett. Avoid: cross-hatch grids, scribbly sketching, a near-empty page.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

graphite hatching and smudging, Al Parker, Robert Fawcett, colored-pencil subject

### Director's notes

Clawd plays the reporter Phil: a fedora with a PRESS card tucked in, a notebook and pencil, standing motionless at the front desk. The first shot is the picture being drawn around it; then two hard cuts to the bystanders, the manager's polite refusal and the glance of a woman turned sideways, with the meaning all in those two faces. The beats land on the cuts. Performance at 1.8x speed.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Wide shot of the hotel lobby, the picture drawn on the spot from construction lines to hatching: Clawd in a fedora with a PRESS card holds his notebook up to the front desk; the camera pulls back slightly. (1.1s)
2. Pushing in from the front-desk manager to his close-up: a slight bow, a smile, with eyes that do not smile, every key still hanging on the rack behind him. (0.8s)
3. Medium shot on a fixed camera: a woman in a fox stole by the elevator, her eyes glancing back toward Clawd. (0.7s)
