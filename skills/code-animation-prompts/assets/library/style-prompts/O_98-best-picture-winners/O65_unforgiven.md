# O65 Unforgiven

- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · Turn of the Century 1990–1999

## Style prompt

Frederic Remington-style Western oil painting. Primary register, for anything before full dark: sunset, a red-gold sky (orange into deep red) in long horizontal strokes, the sun on the horizon, land and figures dark warm silhouettes, thin warm rim sunward. Second register, only after dark: a blue-green nocturne (sky #17333A, moonlit edges #9CC4BC, warm window light #FFCC6A on dark ground #1E1C16, rain). Broad, readable shapes first, then loaded strokes about 1/80 of frame width wide, 3–6 times as long, soft broken ends, each within ±6% brightness of the colour beneath, following the surface. Light canvas texture. Low horizon, sky 50–60% of the frame, figures small to medium. Camera: one slow push from a silhouetted wide to the figure. References: Remington's late nocturnes; cinematographer Jack N. Green (*Unforgiven*, 1992). Avoid: smooth gradients; hard-edged flat bands; shattered shapes; grey figures in warm scenes; wide pans.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Remington's warm-toned plains, Remington's late nocturnes, impasto oil, silhouette of the opening titles

### Director's notes

Clawd plays William Munny: a black wide-brimmed hat, a long raincoat with the collar up, a rifle. The opening silhouette to the grave is a single shot, and he lifts his head just as the push-in arrives; the one hard cut goes to the rainy night, with Clawd riding into town. 1:1 time, no slow motion.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. A push-in from the full sunset (a log cabin, windmill, lone tree, a cross blocking the sun) to Clawd (Munny) at the grave; he lifts his head and holds the rifle across his body. (1.4s)
2. Hard cut to the town of Big Whiskey on a rainy night: Clawd rides a mouse-brown horse down the muddy street past Greeley's saloon with its lit windows, and the camera pushes toward his dripping hat brim. (1.1s)
