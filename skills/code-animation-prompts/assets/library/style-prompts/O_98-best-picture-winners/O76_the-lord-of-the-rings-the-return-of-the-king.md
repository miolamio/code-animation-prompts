# O76 The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King

- Category: O 98 Best Picture Winners · New Millennium 2000–2009
- Looks like: Soft watercolor and pencil landscapes with deep atmospheric perspective, plus an ink-line map on parchment. The film's concept art was painted by Alan Lee and John Howe, so the journey is told the way the trilogy was first drawn.
- Style: Alan Lee-style watercolor + Tolkien map
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 2003

## Style prompt

Fantasy concept-art watercolour and pencil on warm paper. Transparent washes, granulated (pigment speckle, ±6% brightness), a few blooms, darker pooled edges; every contour and ridge carries a dark-brown pencil line (about 1/800 of frame width), short hatching on shadow sides. Aerial perspective: 3–5 receding planes, each paler and cooler. Light is the subject: one strong source per scene (sun, fire, lamp, molten glow) burning through haze, its ember glow on about a quarter of the frame, about half the frame in deep umber and violet shadow. Palette: parchment #E9DCC0, deep umber #3A2418, ember orange #E07A2E, hot red #B23A1E only at the light's core, lavender #B8A8C8 for distance. Journeys may cut to a Tolkien-style map, never the main look. Camera: long lateral pans, slow push-ins to the hero. References: Alan Lee, John Howe. Avoid: smooth digital gradients; vector shapes; pale frames.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

Watercolor and pencil concept illustration, Tolkien-style parchment map, Alan Lee and John Howe

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Frodo: a grey-green cloak collar, a Lorien leaf brooch, the One Ring on a chain. As the decade's showpiece it gets 12 beats: the map lays out the whole road, the beacons carry the camera sweeping across the mountains, the Eye is one short beat of menace, and raising the Ring closes it out. Hard cuts between shots, with long movements inside each shot.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. High-angle view of the parchment map: the camera pushes from the whole sheet into the Shire, then flies along the red dotted line toward Mordor, with Clawd as small as a game piece walking the route. (1.3s)
2. Wide shot: snow mountains at dusk floating above a sea of clouds, the nearest beacon already lit; the camera slowly tracks right and pulls back slightly as the beacons on the ridgeline light up one after another. (1.9s)
3. Close shot: the Eye of Sauron atop Barad-dur; the camera pushes in and the Eye turns, sweeping out a beam. No Clawd. (0.4s)
4. At the fissure of Mount Doom: the camera pushes in from a wide shot of a basalt tongue above the lava to Clawd, the volcano and the Eye behind him, as he lifts the glowing Ring from the edge of the abyss and holds it high. (1.5s)
