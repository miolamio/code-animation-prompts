## Style prompt

Gritty early-1970s documentary look in a detailed 3D world, finished as pushed 16mm. Grade: low saturation, high contrast; neutral-cold greys #7A7C7A, cyan shadows #1E2A2C, warm-brown mid-tones #6E4A34 in brick, wood, vehicles (at least a quarter of the frame), never an overall blue; the one hero (e.g. #D97757) stays saturated. Default overcast, sky near-white, at any time of day; sun through any overhead grid (track, bridge, pier) falls in hard stripes. Grain clumps about 1/700 of frame width, ±8% brightness; gate weave, flicker, dust, occasional faint ghosts of bright lights. Dense clutter fitting the place. Handheld cameras ride the action: a low 18–25 mm lens rushing forward as repeated objects strobe past, a rig looking back at the subject, a corner wide. References: William Friedkin, *The French Connection* (1971); cinematographer Owen Roizman. Avoid: clean CG shine, blue tint, empty places, mushy grain.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays "Popeye" Doyle: a pork-pie hat, angry eyes, both hands on the steering wheel. The chase scene's in-world time runs far faster than screen time, so speeding it up just looks like a faster car. Three hard cuts: a skid at the intersection → a bumper-level charge → the driver, without stopping in between.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Wide shot of the intersection: a train passes overhead as a brown Pontiac fishtails onto the street, rear tires smoking; the camera view widens slowly. (0.8s)
2. The film's famous bumper camera position: an ultra-low camera rushes forward with motion blur, the elevated railway's pillars flashing past one by one. (0.8s)
3. Medium shot from outside the car through the rolled-down window at Clawd as Doyle: pork-pie hat, teeth clenched on the steering wheel, the elevated railway and storefronts sliding past behind him, with in-car shake. (0.9s)
