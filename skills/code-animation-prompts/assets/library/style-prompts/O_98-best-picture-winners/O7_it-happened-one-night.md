## Style prompt

Early-1930s black-and-white rubber-hose cartoon. Characters: noodle limbs, white gloves, pie-cut eyes, round heads; thick-thin ink #141414 (1/400-1/150 of frame width) swelling away from the light (default upper left); fills pure black and white plus at most one light grey for the hard shadow, gloss on blacks. Backgrounds stay mid-grey (30-80%), never pure black/white: graphite lines left in, grey watercolour washes with pooled edges and granulation, paper grain. Only the hero the story centres on has colour (e.g. #D97757): its whole body, or its one key prop if the hero is human. ±1 px gate weave, grain (±4%), flicker, scratches, dust. Actions anticipate, overshoot, settle: squash on landing, stretch on fast moves, eyes pop when surprised, a camera jolt on hits. References: Fleischer's Betty Boop shorts; Disney's Silly Symphonies backgrounds. Avoid: smooth vector lines, grey characters merging with the background. Characters are 3-4 heads tall. Grain runs at 24 fps.

## Notes

### Technique keywords

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Peter: gray fedora, loosened striped tie, a small mustache, and white gloves at the ends of his arms. On beats 3-4 Ellie lifts her skirt, and on beat 5 the car brakes hard (only this moment returns to 1x speed), with Clawd's slack-jawed stare landing exactly on it. Two hard cuts: thumb → two-shot → closing shot.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. Clawd's white-gloved thumb sticking up and his smug face, an empty road behind him, a car coming over the crest of the hill; slow push-in. (0.7s)
2. Two-shot: Clawd thumbing a ride at the roadside, Ellie sitting on a fence hiking her hem up to her calf; gentle pull back. (0.7s)
3. Closing wide shot: the car brakes hard between the two, the driver's eyeballs popping out; Clawd goggles with eyes wide and arms spread, his hat jumping up; the camera jolts at the hard stop. (1.1s)
