# O18 The Lost Weekend

## Style prompt


---

English:

Type every image: one bold monospaced glyph per keystroke, fixed grid, cells 1/150 of frame width wide, twice as tall. Strike 85-95% of cells; bare paper only for highlights. Grey from a key ladder (light . : -, mid = + o, dark X # M); darks (about 30%) are overstrikes like M#. Shapes read as crisp-edged fields, clear silhouettes. Each material repeats its own keys (e.g. I H verticals, _| courses, ~ water). Signs stay legible. Uneven ribbon: half-inked strikes, a few keys always high or low. Paper #EBE6DA, ink #1D1C21; only the hero the story centres on, at least 1/3 of frame height, is struck in red-orange #D4704F, its shadow black. Hard sidelight casts long, crisp, dense shadows. The picture types itself within the first second, then holds. Reference: Paul Smith's typewriter art. Avoid: sparse frames, shapeless streaks.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Don Birnam: a fedora made of typed characters, carrying the typewriter he means to pawn, its four legs stepping in alternation frame by frame. The street appears around him as if typed out by a typewriter; then two quick hard cuts, a close shot and the CLOSED sign, finish telling us that the pawnshop is shut. Performance at 1.8x speed.

### Storyboard in the final film

1. One-point-perspective wide shot under the Third Avenue elevated railway: the street scene is typed onto the paper line by line, Clawd in a fedora walking forward carrying the typewriter; the camera pulls back slightly. (1.3s)
2. Close shot of Clawd: a hat and eyes made of typed characters and the gray typewriter beside him, steel beams behind; slow push-in. (0.6s)
3. Close-up of the J. GOLDMAN LOANS pawnshop sign, the letters assembled from characters, with a CLOSED sign hanging beneath; slow push-in. (0.6s)
